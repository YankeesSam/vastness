# ADR-0007 Earth satellites

Status: accepted (2026-11-26, T-091; OI-4 answered through W-004).

## Context

VN-CAT-007 adds Earth satellites (the ISS, Hubble, Tiangong, a few operational Starlink, GOES-East, GOES-West and other geostationary satellites). Horizons has trajectories for only a few of them, and those it has are short. The public source for every tracked object is the US Space Force catalogue, republished by CelesTrak as general perturbations (GP) mean elements. GP elements are meant for one propagator, SGP4/SDP4, and are only good for days to weeks around their epoch. The app must stay static (ADR-0005): it cannot fetch elements at run time.

## Decision

- **Source (VN-DAT-009).** CelesTrak GP data, fetched at build time by `tools/gp` by NORAD catalogue number in OMM JSON: `https://celestrak.org/NORAD/elements/gp.php?CATNR=<n>&FORMAT=JSON`. The OMM keywords are CCSDS 502.0-B-3 (OBJECT_NAME, OBJECT_ID, NORAD_CAT_ID, EPOCH in UTC, MEAN_MOTION rev/day, ECCENTRICITY, INCLINATION, RA_OF_ASC_NODE, ARG_OF_PERICENTER, MEAN_ANOMALY in degrees, BSTAR in 1/earth radii, MEAN_MOTION_DOT, MEAN_MOTION_DDOT; REF_FRAME TEME, TIME_SYSTEM UTC, MEAN_ELEMENT_THEORY SGP4).
- **Politeness (CelesTrak usage policy, read 2026-11-26).** CelesTrak updates GP data once every 2 hours and returns HTTP 403 for a second download of the same data within an update window; it asks for one download per update, compressed responses, `celestrak.org` (not `.com`), and that machine clients stop at the first non-200 response (15 minutes after a 500 or 503) and stay under 50 HTTP errors in 2 hours. So `tools/gp` uses the same polite client and cache as Horizons (one request in flight, at least 1 s apart, project User-Agent, SHA-256 cache key, `--offline`), never re-downloads a cached response younger than 2 hours, asks for gzip, and stops the run at the first non-200 response without retrying (a 500 or 503 is reported with the 15-minute wait). About 20 satellites make about 20 requests per refresh. JSON is about three times the size of CSV; at this count that is a few kilobytes, and JSON keeps the OMM keywords VN-DAT-009 names.
- **Terms.** CelesTrak makes GP data freely available and states no licence or credit requirement; the data derive from US Space Force tracking (space-track.org). DATA_SOURCES records this, and the About box credits "Orbital elements from CelesTrak (celestrak.org)" as a courtesy. Provenance per satellite: element epoch, retrieval date, source URL, element set number.
- **Propagator (VN-CAT-007).** Our own SGP4/SDP4 in `src/core/sgp4/`, written from the published algorithm (Hoots and Roehrich, Spacetrack Report No. 3, 1980, with the corrections of Vallado, Crawford, Hujsak and Kelso, "Revisiting Spacetrack Report #3", AIAA 2006-6753) in the WGS-72 constants GP elements are fitted with, and checked against that paper's published verification vectors (near-Earth, deep-space resonant and GEO cases). Not `satellite.js`: it would be a new runtime dependency (ADR-0001's allowlist) and does not follow the core's rules (float64 outputs into caller arrays, no allocation per call, no globals). The port is about 1,000 lines and its tests are the reference vectors, not our own output.
- **Frames (ADR-0003).** SGP4 gives TEME (true equator, mean equinox of date) km and km/s from Earth's centre. The core turns them into J2000 ecliptic: TEME to true of date by the equation of the equinoxes, true to mean of date by IAU 1980 nutation (its 18 largest terms: truncation under about 0.1 arcsecond, 20 m at GEO, far below SGP4's own error), mean of date to J2000 by IAU 1976 precession, then the fixed J2000 obliquity rotation. Time arguments come from TDB seconds since J2000 through the existing UTC and TT conversions (SGP4 counts minutes from the UTC element epoch). Satellites are stored as children of Earth (399), like the Moon (VN-EPH-009).
- **Validity.** GP elements degrade by roughly a kilometre a day for low orbits. Within 14 days of the element epoch the satellite is drawn at its SGP4 position, with the epoch age in the panel; beyond that it is drawn on an illustrative orbit (the osculating ellipse at 14 days, as for orbiters, VN-EPH-011), labelled as such, never at an extrapolated exact position. Refreshing elements is part of the refresh job (VN-DAT-006, T-047).

## Consequences

- No new runtime dependency; the SGP4 port is ours to maintain, held to the reference vectors.
- A build's satellites are only exact for two weeks around its elements; the refresh workflow keeps a published build current.
- ISS has a Horizons trajectory (-125544), which T-093 uses as an independent check of propagator and frames for one day.
